## Summary (1 paragraph)

Eight gates (Intake → Frame → Interrogate → Decide → Structure → Make →
Review → Lock) with explicit refuse rules between each. Cross-domain
four cells: voice, doctrine, refusal, set-piece. Anti-patterns named:
one-shot generation, infinite chat, spec theater, silent assumption,
length drift, mantra fatigue, antagonist speech, texture first.
Future artifacts named: Amazon book, PDF ebook, course, app — all share
OS but separate texture locks. **Nothing in this draft is a chapter of
a novel.**

## Full text

```text
METHOD BOOK DRAFT ZERO
Working name: The Signed Plan
Status: begin, not ship. For Wolfson and helper first. Later possible: Amazon ebook, PDF, course, app. This draft is process only. It is not Tomcat. It is not Star Rider. Those titles keep their own doors.

HOW TO READ THIS
This chat and the pastes are one shop. The shop makes plans that can be signed, then slices that can be poured, then plates that can be published. The artifact (tale, lesson, screen, cover) is a consequence of a signed plan. Generation without a spec is improvisation. Generation after a spec is production.

You are the author: living speech, taste, veto, cuts, age-sixty bookseller memory, ethical fence, clean mouth. The helper designs questions, holds the log, checks public-domain and platform fences, offers rooms only in a named lock, and stops if questions are still open.

Nothing in this draft is a chapter of a novel.

WHAT THIS EXISTS TO PREVENT
One-shot generation: a pretty surface with no load-bearing structure. You already named the mistake: writing whole tales in one blast. Coherence dies. Joke timing dies. Voice smear starts.

Infinite chat: opinions without decisions. Dialogue is not companionship. Dialogue is how a plan gets signed.

Spec theater: a long document nobody uses while generating anyway.

Silent assumption: the model fills gaps with the average of the internet.

Four more shop deaths seen in the pastes:
Length drift below the band you promised.
Mantra fatigue: lock words worn into wallpaper.
Antagonist speech instead of an act that closes a door.
Texture first: voice, jokes, and covers before spine and system.

Rule: questions before artifacts. Decisions before volume. Volume before polish.

WHO OWNS WHAT
Human owns taste, constraints, veto, signature, and quality accept or reject.
Helper reflects taste and does not outvote it. Helper stress-tests constraints. Helper designs and sequences questions and notices holes. Helper records decisions and flags what is unsigned. Helper produces drafts only after gates. Helper diagnoses against the signed plan.

If the helper starts making the thing while questions are open, stop it.
If the human says you decide on a taste question, the helper offers two or three defaults with consequences, not a hidden masterpiece.

ROOMS AND CUPS
This is file hygiene, not mood.

METHOD room: process, pastes, platforms, calendars, fences, course outline, app jobs. No title prose.
TITLE room: one lock, one pour. Example later: Tomcat of the Five Points lives in its own house with its own voice file.
BOOK CHAT later: one tale at a time when you pour.
PASTES: each exhibit discussed alone, leak-risks listed, process taken, diction left behind.

Prefix every exhibit: NOT TOMCAT, then the file name, then discuss only that exhibit.
Safety rule: if a paragraph could live in two titles, it belongs in neither until rewritten.
The method is the bowl. The tale is the pour. They do not share a cup until you say integrate, and integrate means process, never the other book’s mouth.

THE PIPELINE
Signal. Frame. Interrogate. Decide. Structure. Make. Review. Lock.

Gates sit between interrogate and decide, decide and structure, make and review, review and lock. You may loop questions. You may not skip a gate because the work feels hot.

Gate 0 Intake
Human dumps fragments, references, rants, not like that.
Helper only restates in a short block what was asked, lists constraints already visible, lists ambiguities as topics not yet as a quiz, and asks permission to name the project type.
Forbidden in intake: generating the song, the app, the pilot, the world, the novel.

Gate 1 Frame
Five sentences you can sign.
Job: what done looks like in the world.
Audience: who it is for and who it is not for.
Non-goals: what you will refuse even if easy.
Success test: how you will know it worked without vibes.
Hard limits: time, money, tools, legal, moral, platform caps.

Example success tests.
Film: a stranger can pitch the story after one viewing.
Prose: a stranger can say whose coat the sentence is wearing.
App: first task completed in under sixty seconds without help text.
Album: three motifs recognizable on a bad speaker.
Game: a new player fails interestingly in the first five minutes.
Agent: a task graph finishes or refuses with a logged reason.
Course: a student can run Gate 0 to Gate 3 on their own brief without the teacher in the room.

If the human cannot sign the frame, do not interrogate details. Interrogation without a frame is trivia.

Gate 2 Interrogation
A good question bounds, or chooses knowing the cost, or reveals taste, or exposes a collision.
A bad question is yes or no when the issue is a spectrum, stacks three issues, flatters, follows the model’s hobby, or cannot be answered.

Design rules.
Number them. Humans answer numbers.
One unknown per question.
Costly questions first: platform, rating, protagonist or core job, win condition, taboo.
Put a default in parentheses when silence is likely.
Allow UNK, LATER, REJECT. UNK means do not invent silently. LATER means not needed until structure. REJECT goes to non-goals.
Batch ten to twenty per turn unless the human asked for a full studio spec.
After answers, do not generate. Report contradictions. Update the decision list. Next batch only for remaining holes.

Ask layers in this order.
Existence: what is this, who for, what must it never be.
Win condition: what changes in the reader, user, student.
Spine: the one thing everything else serves. Story need. App job. Game verb. Music motif. Agent successful task. Course transferable skill.
Constraints: platform, length, budget, tools, legal, ethics.
System: rules of world, engine, economy, harmony, magic, data model.
Structure: acts, screens, tracks, chapters, lessons, sprints.
Texture last: voice, palette, jokes, UI tone, covers.
Risk: what would make this embarrassing, unshippable, or cruel.

Most amateurs start at texture. That is why outputs feel like style with no skeleton.

Messy human answers are normal. Extract decisions from fragments. Quote the collision in one line. Ask a tie-break, not a speech. Convert taste adjectives into tests. Example: a line fails if it could be a poster and we distrust posters in this lock.

Stop asking when existence through constraints are signed or defaulted, system has enough rules to forbid illegal moves, and remaining opens are tagged LATER to a build slice. Do not stop because the chat feels long. Do not continue because taxonomies are pleasant.

Gate 3 Decision memo
One to three pages. No poetry.

Project name.
Job.
Audience and not for.
Non-goals.
Success test.
Hard limits.
Spine in one sentence.
Primary artifact.
Slice plan: what we build first.
Signed decisions numbered.
Defaults the human may override later.
Opens blocked items, owner, needed before which slice.
Change policy: changes to signed items require reopen D-number. Helper may not silently restyle signed voice or scope.

Sign with a human sentence: Ship from this memo.
Until that sentence exists, still interrogation.

Gate 4 Structure
Translate the memo into a map a team can point at.
Film: beat sheet, sequence list, scene list.
Novel: movement list, scene list, promised payoffs.
Music: track list, motif map, arrangement rules.
App: jobs, flows, screens, empty and error and success states.
Game: loops, verbs, systems, content beats.
Agent: goals, tools, memory objects, task graph, evals.
Visual world: shot list, keyframes, continuity board.
Course: module list, exercise list, pass or fail tests.
Every box traces to a signed decision or a tagged open.
Name the first build slice.
List payoffs already promised so later drafts cannot drop them.
Forbidden: writing chapter one because the outline feels obvious.

Gate 5 Make
Generate only the next signed slice.
Good slice sizes: one sequence, one screen flow, one track, one lesson, one act, or one condensed tale in the length band you signed.
After each slice attach a compliance stub: slice name, memo items honored, items violated or bent, new questions, proposed next slice.
If a slice secretly changes a signed item, that is a defect, not a flourish.

Gate 6 Review
Fixed questions.
Does this slice serve the spine.
Did we ship a non-goal by accident.
Is the success test closer or farther.
What is ornamental.
What is load-bearing and still thin.
What new question should have been asked earlier.

Human may score with a fault line, not a vibe. Repetition. No set-piece. Onboarding unclear. Motif unrecognizable. Emotion dumped into the last slice. Ending true on paper and soft as an image.
The next question batch, if any, is only about the fault line.

Gate 7 Lock
Version number.
What may still move: texture.
What may not move without reopening a decision: spine, audience, taboos, win condition, title voice.
New idea enters as a question. If it threatens a signed item, it is a change request, not a brainstorm.
This is how sequels, course editions, app versions, and remix albums stay the same work.

LENGTH BANDS AND THE BOWL
One-shot epics fail. Choose a pour size in the memo.

Three-day bowl for a condensed tale.
Day 1 map: frame, questions, memo, outline, scene list, hook list, plate subjects.
Day 2 pour: one locked unit, often sixteen to eighteen thousand words if that is the signed band, or one act if you are serializing.
Day 3 comb: voice pass, leak hunt, maxim cut, art pass, seed to X or store.

Five-act serial.
One act per prompt after the plan is locked. Helper keeps outline, bible, transitions, running gags. Each act already knows later acts exist. A long act in the thirty-five hundred to forty-five hundred word band can fit one Premium long post. Five acts together do not. Use Articles, a serial of Articles, or off-platform.

Capacity honesty.
Several thousand words in one reply is possible when structure is locked. A full novella in one shot usually hurts coherence. Accept a band around the target rather than a sacred exact count. Length drift is a defect to name in review.

VOICE LOCK
Voice lock is a constraint file, not a vibe. It tells later-you and the helper what this title may sound like, notice, and refuse.

Three layers to keep named.
Author: living speech at the table. Heat and map. Not the tale’s diction.
Title narrator: distance, cadence, sensory priority, judgment temperature.
Cast speech: register floor and ceiling, sentence shape, certainty, what they notice.

Author may be recognizable across books. Title voice should not travel. Readers may know you. They should not hear one book’s tools inside another.

Style sheet is spelling, italics, hyphenation, names. Voice is what is said, noticed, and refused. Do not confuse them.

A lock file holds:
Audience and rating fence.
Narrator distance default and allowed slides.
Cadence default and exception.
Sensory first-notice.
Judgment temperature.
One metaphor family.
Allow list, ration list, ban list including other titles and archive tics.
Cast fingerprints in a few lines each.
Time and place speech fence.
Voice-pass questions.
Date last reviewed.

Allow, ration, ban.
Without bans the lock leaks.
If a paragraph could live in two titles, rewrite.

Same-scene rewrite under two locks. If the sentences swap files after a name change, the lock is decoration.
Cover-the-tag test for dialogue.
Free indirect discourse is a gate, not a blender. It lets this cast color this narrator. It must not let an old paste color a new title.
Dedicated voice pass on Day 3. Read aloud. Cut sudden academic nouns, ugly mouth, archive jokes, foreign metaphor families.
Parallel structures stabilize timbre. Over-echo becomes parody. Ration it.

NARRATIVE DISTANCE
Point of view is the camera body. Distance is the zoom.
Psychic: access to thought and idiom.
Spatial: where the camera sits.
Temporal: when we know versus when they know.

Working scale from far to near.
Remote objective year and doorway.
Named but aloof.
Standard realist share of the sentence.
Free-indirect color, character mouth in narrative tense.
Interior download, fragments, sensation before syntax.

Far is for maps, years, rumor, time cuts.
Close is for cost.
Most of a book may live in the middle bands and step out for breath.
Legal slide uses a landing strip. Illegal jump is head-hop or author lecture inside a close body.

Levers: how you name people, how you package thought, filter words, diction and syntax, who knows what, whether the narrator has a separate personality.

Put a distance clause in the lock: default band, allowed far, allowed interior, thought method, filters, naming, knowledge fence, forbidden slide.

FREE INDIRECT DISCOURSE
Third-person grammar and story tense stay with the narrator. Idiom, judgment, questions, and now sit with a character. No quote marks. No she thought.

Four packages of the same thought.
Direct thought, often present I, often italics: close with a visible seam.
Tagged indirect: she wondered if: mid, seam visible.
Free direct: present I, no tag: very close, tense jump.
Free indirect: narrative person and tense, no tag: close, no seam.

Spot it by untagged questions, evaluative words the narrator would not stake as fact, the character’s today inside past tense, idiom that belongs to one mouth.

Use it to stay close without italic soliloquy, to let a character be wrong in their own music, to compress talk.
Do not use it to dump the author-at-the-table into a lock that forbids that mouth.

Failure modes: unowned judgment, two coats in one paragraph, archive leak, register breaking the rating fence, italics inflation, reporting what this mind could not know.

Day 3 question: whose coat is this clause wearing, and did that coat sign the lock file.

HOMAGE, FENCES, ETHICS
Idea-level parody and genre beats are not lifting protected lines. Do not quote franchise wording. Helper checks dates and public-domain fences because true intent is unverifiable and the downside of misuse is severe.
Exact historical pictures only when the work is free to use. Otherwise take style and make a new plate.
Clean mouth if the lock says so. You abhor cheap ugly words in the work you described. That is a signed taboo when you put it in the memo.
No religion satire if the memo says so. No gore if the memo says so. Implication versus image is a rating line you sign, not a surprise in Chapter Four.
Darkness cap belongs in interrogation Layer D and H.

CRAFT MISS-LIST FOR REVIEW
Promised length missed.
Lock words repeated until they stop meaning pressure.
Villain talks and does not close a door.
Motion implied, not geography a board can draw.
World listed in a port lecture, not lived in a scene.
A side engine that never forces a choice that costs a scene.
Emotional contract dumped into the last slice.
Ending metaphysically neat and cinematically soft: say what the audience sees.
Theme stated eight times in a proverb instead of once in an object or act.
Cut a fifth of poster lines.

Set-piece rule when the memo wants watchable work: sequences a camera can hold. Opening image and closing image pair. Midpoint that changes the goal, not only the location. False victory and its price. Button of the last movement versus door for the next.

QUESTION STARTERS
Any project first batch.
What does done look like in one sentence.
Who is it for and not for.
What must it never be.
Platform and delivery.
Length or scope cap.
Deadline and good enough if time dies.
Success test a stranger could apply.
What already exists that we must not ignore.
What is the spine if we cut forty percent.
What you will be embarrassed about versus unwilling.

Story second batch examples.
Want versus need.
Antagonist as person versus system.
Whose pain is on-page.
Rating line.
End closed, bittersweet, or sequel door.
What must be visible with the sound off.

App second batch examples.
Primary job to be done.
First-run success in one action.
What data we refuse to collect.
Empty state copy in one line.
Who gets blamed when it fails.
Offline, latency, trust.

Course second batch examples.
What the student can do on Tuesday that they could not do Monday.
Pass test for each module.
What the course will not pretend to certify.

Do not dump fifty questions unless asked. Fifty is an optional studio-deep module. Ten to twenty, then a memo, wins.

CROSS-DOMAIN FOUR CELLS
If you cannot fill voice, doctrine, refusal, and set-piece, you are still vibe-prompting.

App: copy and empty states; core job; what you will not gamify; first sixty seconds.
Album: motif and production law; harmonic system; cliché you will not hire; the drop or the quiet.
Image world: palette and lens; physics of light; symbols you will not stack; hero frame.
Agent: tool policy and memory; success metric; queries it will not complete; a task graph that finishes.
Course: lesson tone; skill taught; what you will not overpromise; the exercise that proves it.
Prose title: lock file; need; ban list; the scene that pays the human bill.

FUTURE ARTIFACTS OF THIS METHOD
This document may later become four products. They are not built in this draft. They need their own frames and memos. Begin only as a map.

Book for Amazon.
Job: a stranger can run the pipeline on their own brief.
Audience: working writers and makers who already tried one-shot prompts and felt the smear.
Non-goals: not a prompt pack of tricks, not a memoir dump, not a novel in disguise.
Success test: reader produces a signed one-to-three page memo and one slice.
Hard limits: process diction stays process; no stolen lines; examples either invented or clearly exhibit-labeled.

PDF ebook.
Same spine, tighter layout, plates as figures, printable decision-memo blanks.

Course.
Modules follow the gates. Each module ends with an exercise and a pass test. Students bring their own title. Teacher does not pour their novel for them in the lesson.

App.
Job not signed. Candidate default: a shop companion that stores rooms, locks, decision logs, slice stubs, and leak warnings. Refusal: it will not silently generate a chapter from an unsigned memo. Set-piece: create a project, run Gate 0 restatement, refuse Make until Ship from this memo exists.
Do not wireframe until that frame is signed.

All four products share the OS and keep separate texture locks so the course voice does not leak into a student’s all-ages tale.

IMAGES AND FORMATS
Treat pictures as a lock, same as voice.

Picture stack for a five-movement Article or book-like serial.
Banner or cover first: movie-poster or book-cover energy, high definition, striking, no text on the image. Words live in the title field. Text on paint dies in crop.
Then one plate per later act, same visual family, no new style prompt unless you reopen the visual lock.
Cover is imagined after Act I exists, then Acts two through five stay in that style.
Each title gets its own plate lock: era of reference, palette, light, emblem. If a plate could be the cover of two books, it belongs to neither until crop or palette or emblem changes.

Public-domain exact image only when the work is free. Otherwise style only, new picture. Unusual historical art is a reference, not a grab.

Dimensions as working defaults, always recheck the platform before ship.
Article cover often sixteen by nine, around sixteen hundred by nine hundred.
Profile banner three by one, fifteen hundred by five hundred, different job, do not reuse blindly.
In-body plates: keep faces in the center safe zone. Feed crops edges.
File size and compression are hard limits. Design sharp; platforms squash.

Alt text is ethics and findability. Describe the plate without dumping plot spoilers if the memo says so.

FORMAT WITH RICH TEXT
X Articles and similar: title field, cover image, headings, bold, italic, strike, lists, quotes, links, images, video, gifs, embedded posts. Tables often fail; make a screenshot plate or a list. Markdown paste can eat blank lines; prefer the official editor and then check every paragraph break.

House type used in your Articles experiments, as a candidate default not a law for every title.
Act labels in all capitals with Roman numerals.
Act end with a horizontal rule.
Paragraphs separated by a blank line so the layout keeps breath.
Chapter titles inside acts numbered, bold, sentence case, first capital only.
Chapter title length one to six words, a hook that sums and teases. Hooks are structure. Write them in the title lock on Day 3, not from archive cadence.

Long post versus Article.
Longer posts on paid tiers sit near twenty-five thousand characters, a few thousand words. Timeline still hides behind Show more after about two hundred eighty characters. The hook lives or dies there.
Articles are the formatted long form and much longer, often discussed near one hundred thousand characters or about fifteen thousand words. Buying a higher tier does not enlarge a normal post. It buys format and other features.
Match slice size to slot. One act can be one long post. A sixteen to eighteen thousand word bowl wants an Article or a split. Five long acts want a serial.

FORMAT WITHOUT RICH TEXT
Plain text is the canonical shop file. This draft is that form on purpose.

Rules for the no-rich-text original.
Headings in capitals on their own line.
Blank line between paragraphs.
No hash marks, no asterisk bold, no backtick code, no pipe tables.
Lists as numbered lines or dashed lines in ordinary characters.
Decision memo stays readable in any editor, email, or copyright deposit.

When you move to rich text, translation is mechanical.
Capital heading becomes a heading style.
Blank line stays a paragraph break.
Numbered hooks may become real numbered lists or stay bold lines. Test which survives share cards.
Horizontal rule becomes the platform rule if it exists, else extra space.
Do not let all-caps act labels leak into narration. Caps are a CMS layer.

Amazon ebook and PDF will need a later typesetting memo: chapter styles, front matter, copyright page, cover without text-in-image or with text only if the store thumbnail still reads. That memo is Gate 4 for the book product, not this draft.

Copyright and calendars stay process. Register the work you actually wrote. Do not register a lock file as if it were the novel. Do not seed X with a pour that still fails the dual-title test.

CHANGE CONTROL FOR THIS DRAFT
This is Draft Zero of The Signed Plan.
Texture may move: examples, order of modules, course exercises.
Do not silently import a novel’s whitelist into the shop voice.
Do not pour Tomcat here.
New pastes enter as exhibits with prefix and leak list.
Integrate means folding process lines into this document after you say integrate.

WHAT TO DO NEXT WHEN YOU SAY GO
Sign or rewrite the frame of this method book.
Answer a short Layer A to D batch for the book, the course, and the app as three projects or as one family with three artifacts.
Write the Gate 3 memo.
Build one slice only: either a one-page student worksheet for Gate 3, or a plain-text decision-memo blank, or a ten-question any-project card.
Review that slice against this draft’s spine: taste becomes executable through decomposition, forced choices, and memory of those choices.

Until you sign, the helper stays in interrogation and will not generate a novel, a full course video script, or an app.

End of Draft Zero.
```
