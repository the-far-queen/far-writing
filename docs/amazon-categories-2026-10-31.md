## THE FILTER, and it is a filter about PEOPLE

**"Who would buy a book and why."**

That single question decides a category, and it is a harder test than
"is the category big". A category can be enormous and still fail the
test, because size is not the same as a buyer with a job to do.

**THE TEST, as four questions:**

1. **WHO** is the buyer? Not "people who like X". A person with a
   situation: a student with an exam date, an owner with a price to
   set, a parent with a teenager, a beginner with a plot.
2. **WHY** do they buy? What JOB does the book do for them? A book is
   bought to do a job, and the job is what the title has to name.
3. **IS THE TITLE THEIR SEARCH?** If the buyer types "how to stop my
   dog barking", the title must be close to that. A title that is
   beautiful and unsearchable is a title nobody finds.
4. **IS IT EVERGREEN?** A book that sells once and dies is a business.
   A book that sells every year is an asset. The gurus' whole
   argument is about the second kind.

**AND THE ANSWER MUST NAME A JOB, NOT A FEELING.** "Books about love"
is not a job. "How to rebuild trust with someone you have hurt" is.

## WHY LAW AND MEDICINE ARE SKIPPED, and it is not size

**LAW.** The buyer's job is passing the bar, and that job is sold as a
COURSE, not a book: BarPrep Hero, Themis, Adaptibar. A book is the
optional part of the purchase. And the money in law books is legal
liability for the author and the publisher on every page.

**MEDICINE.** Same shape and worse. US medical exam prep is dominated
by UWorld, Lecturio, Picmonic and Osmosis, sold as bundles at
$200-$400 with a subscription and a question bank behind it. A book
cannot beat a question bank. **And our own supply is the wrong
market:** the 182,822-question medical bank is Indian-exam material
("All India exam", G.I.T, AIIMS-style topics), so it does not
address the US boards market at all. It is a large, real, and
*different* inventory — and "different" is the honest word.

**HEALTH IS RICH BUT EASILY BANNED.** Bobby's own call, and it is
right: the health category is rich in demand and rich in claims. A
health book that makes a claim is a regulatory target (FTC's health
claim substantiation, FDA on structure/function claims), and the
enforcement lands on the LISTING. The category where the marginal book
is most likely to be pulled is the one where every book makes claims.

**THE PATTERN ACROSS ALL THREE, and it is the useful part:** they are
the categories where the buyer's job is satisfied by a COURSE or a
SUBSCRIPTION, or where the content carries a claim that can be
enforced against us. In every case the book is not where the money is
made.

### 2. BUSINESS

- **The buyer's job:** do a money job. Price a service. Hire the first
  person. Raise money. Get the first customer. Go to market.
- **Why it is keywordable:** the job HAS a name, and the name is
  typed. Nobody searches "business insights"; they search "how to
  price my freelance work" or "how to raise my first 100k".
- **Why we can win it:** a business book has to be specific and
  actionable, and that is a CRAFT, not a category. It is the one
  category where writing quality is directly the product.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Price Your Freelance Work" (6)
  - "How to Raise Your First Round" (6)
  - "How to Hire Your First Employee" (6)
  - "How to Find Your First Customer" (6)

### 3. SELF-HELP

- **The buyer's job:** change a behaviour they have ALREADY tried to
  change. That is why the search phrase is nearly always "how to
  stop..." or "how to build...".
- **The voice, and this is the difference.** Bobby, 2026-10-31: *"we
  write self help in voice of public domain old authors who were good
  at storytelling and communication"*. So the TITLE is keyword-shaped
  and the BOOK is written in a literary voice. The title does the
  finding; the prose does the convincing. Two layers, both ours.
- **AND THE CATALOG IS THE MATERIAL, NOT THE SUBJECT.** The canon is
  1800-1910 public domain -- Melville, Austen, Gilman, the Wells. It
  is not there to be copied; it is there because those writers knew
  how to make a person act, and that is the thing slop cannot fake.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Stop Procrastinating Forever" (5)
  - "How to Build Discipline That Lasts" (6)
  - "How to Think Clearly Under Pressure" (6)

### 4. GARDENING

- **The buyer's job:** grow a specific plant in a specific place.
  That is a PHRASE, not a feeling: "how to grow tomatoes in
  containers", "how to fix clay soil".
- **Why it is evergreen:** the gardener is always starting again, and
  the beginner is always new.
- **Low claim risk, low cost, and the cover is showable** -- a garden
  sells with a photograph, and far-art's sheet schema can make one.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Grow Tomatoes in Containers" (6)
  - "How to Fix Clay Soil Fast" (6)
  - "What to Plant in Shaded Beds" (6)

### 5. PETS AND ANIMAL CARE

- **The buyer's job:** stop one specific behaviour. "How to stop my
  dog barking", "how to stop my cat scratching the sofa".
- **Why it is keywordable:** the job has a phrase, and the phrase is
  the title.
- **Why it recurs:** a dog's behaviour changes with age and season, so
  the same buyer comes back, and a second dog is a second sale.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Stop Your Dog Barking" (6)
  - "How to Stop Your Cat Scratching" (6)
  - "How to Stop a Dog Pulling on Leash" (8 -- tight)

### 6. PARENTING

- **The buyer's job:** get through a stage. The stage has a name and
  a date: toddlers, sleep, teenagers, the first school year.
- **The phrases are the ages, and the ages are the market:**
  "toddler sleep", "how to talk to a teenager".
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Talk to a Teenager" (5)
  - "How to Get Your Toddler to Sleep" (7 -- tight)
  - "How to Handle a Temper Tantrum" (6)

### 7. RELIGION AND SPIRITUALITY -- where we are the experts

- **The buyer's job:** a PRACTICE, not a topic. "prayer for anxiety",
  "how to meditate", "understanding the book of psalms".
- **WHY THIS IS THE ONE NOBODY ELSE CAN COPY, and it is the whole
  reason Bobby built the sacred library:** 30 years of sitting 5 hours
  every other day, 7-hour singing, Tsongkhapa at Jonang and Nala
  Gompa, 17,000 feet, teaching kung fu internally. **The supply of
  lived practice is the barrier, and we have it.** A writer can copy a
  technique; a writer cannot copy 30 years in a monastery.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Meditate for Beginners" (5)
  - "How to Pray When You Cannot" (6)
  - "How to Sit With Difficult Emotions" (6)

### 8. DATING AND RELATIONSHIPS

- **The buyer's job:** get one specific outcome, or communicate with
  one specific person.
- **CAUTION, and it is a real one:** this category is where AI slop
  concentrates, because the phrases are easy and the advice is cheap.
  The 14,419-book study found AI-heavy books take 11.3% of revenue.
  So this category is only worth entering with the two things we have
  and slop does not: a specific mechanism, and a voice.
- **THE 4-6 WORD TITLES:**
  - "How to Rebuild Trust After Betrayal" (6)
  - "How to Text Someone You Like" (7 -- tight)
  - "How to Fight Without Hurting" (6)

### 9. COOKBOOKS -- partly, and the reason is the cover

- **The buyer's job:** cook a specific dish. The phrase is exact: "one

- **WHY ONLY PARTLY:** a cookbook sells on the PHOTOGRAPH, not the
  title. Pedruzzi and Ken both say the cover is the click. And a
  cookbook without a cover pipeline cannot ship. **So: cookbooks are
  deferred until far-art's sheet schema has a cover use-case.** The

  - "100 One-Pot Meals for Beginners" (6)

## THE TITLE GENERATOR'S RULE, and it is the deliverable

**A TITLE IS 4-6 WORDS AND IT IS A PHRASE THE BUYER TYPES.**

The patterns that work, and every one of them is a phrase:

| pattern | example | the job it names |
|---|---|---|
| "How to [verb] [object]" | How to Price Your Freelance Work | the most common buyer job |
| "How to [verb] [object] [qualifier]" | How to Stop Your Dog Barking | the specific case |
| "[N] [subject] [noun] [and answers]" | 500 Chemistry Questions and Answers | the exam job |
| "[subject] [noun] for [audience]" | Biology Study Guide for Beginners | the audience |
| "[N] [thing] [thing] [thing]" | 100 One-Pot Meals for Beginners | the list job |
| "How to [verb] [object] for [audience]" | How to Meditate for Beginners | the audience |

**WHAT NEVER WORKS, and it is the temptation:** a title that is a
beautiful sentence. Nobody types "the quiet discipline of becoming".
Nobody types "a practice in attention". **They type "how to meditate
for beginners".** The prose carries the voice; the title carries the
job.

**AND THE 4-6 WORD LIMIT IS A DISCIPLINE, not a constraint.** A title
longer than 6 words is a title trying to carry two jobs. One title,
one job, one phrase.

## THE MEASUREMENT NOTE, and it is a limitation

All the Amazon counts above were measured through a browser session,
because **Amazon's `/s` endpoint returns HTTP 503 to a plain
`urllib` request** (MEASURED 2026-10-31: the homepage returns 200,
every `/s` search returns 503, from every header combination tried --
browser User-Agent, Accept-Encoding, sec-fetch headers, session-id
cookie). And this machine's delivery country is **Nepal**, so
Amazon's bestseller grid for that store returns *"Sorry, there are no
Best Sellers available in this category."* The SEARCH works and the
grid does not.

**So the counts are from the search endpoint and the grid is not
reachable from this store.** Any future tool must say which endpoint
it used, because a demand number with no endpoint behind it is not a
measurement.
